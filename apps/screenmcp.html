Overview

Give an agent eyes, not the run of your desktop

Screen sharing with an AI is usually all or nothing. ScreenMCP makes it one source at a time, chosen by you, revocable in one click, and written down.

You pick exactly one monitor, one window, or a region you drag. Until you pick, nothing is shared. The chosen source stays open as a stream, so there is no operating-system picker popping up for every request the model makes.

On top of that source you can draw redaction masks to black out whatever should not leave your machine, and highlights to point the model at what matters. The masks are applied before the frame is resized, hashed, encoded or written to the audit trail, so the hidden pixels never reach the model at all.

One toggle moves between three modes. Off is the stop gate: clients stay connected but every call comes back refused. Read-only lets the model look. Interactive, on Windows, lets it click and type inside that one source, and if a click arrives while you are still in read-only, ScreenMCP surfaces itself and waits for you to approve rather than failing quietly.

One source, chosen by you

A monitor, a window, or a dragged region. Nothing is shared until you choose it.

Redaction and highlights

Mask secrets, point at what matters. Masks are applied before anything is encoded or stored.

Three access modes

Off, read-only, or interactive, with an emergency stop that revokes everything at once.

Approval, not silent failure

A click that arrives in read-only mode waits for your explicit yes, up to two minutes.

Change-aware capture

Watching an app for progress returns text until the picture actually changes, so idle screens cost no vision tokens.

A local audit trail

Every request logged with client, source, action and outcome, next to reviewable thumbnails. Typed text is never stored.

Localhost only, bearer authenticated, open source under MIT. Interactive mode is Windows for now; look and wait-for-change work everywhere.
