Records both sides of a call, then writes the transcript and the summary for you.

Overview

The call, written down

Written for Google Meet and Teams calls in Czech and English. It records what comes out of your speakers and what goes into your microphone, and takes it from there.

The two sides are captured as separate tracks and mixed when you stop, each with its own volume, so a quiet caller does not disappear under your own voice. Level meters run while recording, which is how you find out the wrong microphone is selected before the meeting rather than after it.

One click sends a recording for transcription: the language is detected, the text is split by speaker and timestamped, and the result lands next to the recording as a Markdown file. If you have the Claude CLI installed, another click has it read the transcript and write a summary beside it, in whatever shape your prompt asks for.

It also dictates. Hold the hotkey anywhere, speak, let go, and the text appears where your caret is, with your clipboard restored afterwards. And during a call it can put live subtitles under the level meters, the original above and the translation below, without touching the recording itself.

Both sides, two tracks

Speakers and microphone captured separately and mixed on stop, each with its own level.

Transcript with speakers

Language detection, speaker labels and timestamps, saved next to the recording.

A written summary

The Claude CLI reads the transcript and writes the summary; the prompt is yours to change.

Push-to-talk dictation

Hold, speak, release, and the text lands where you were typing.

Live translated subtitles

The other side's words appear as they speak, original above, translation below.

Honest about cost

Live subtitles stream the whole call and the recording is transcribed again afterwards. You are told, and it is off by default.

Open source under MIT. Written and used on Windows; macOS and Linux builds ship with the caveats listed in the repository.
